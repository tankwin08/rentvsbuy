# Rent vs Buy Calculator – How the Numbers Work

This calculator helps you compare renting vs buying a home. You adjust the sliders, and it shows you:
- Monthly costs of owning (non‑recoverable)
- How many years until buying becomes cheaper than renting (break‑even)
- What your money could grow to if you rent and invest the savings in the S&P 500
- How much home equity you build after a certain number of years

All numbers can be adjusted for inflation (real vs nominal values).

---

## Key terms (plain English)

**Unrecoverable costs** – Money you never get back. For owners: mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, maintenance. For renters: just the rent cheque.

**Owner premium** – If owners pay $2,500/month and renters pay $2,000/month, the premium is $500. That $500 is what renters can invest each month.

**Transaction frictions** – One‑time costs: closing costs when you buy, real estate commission when you sell. You need home appreciation to overcome these.

**Break‑even year** – The number of years after which your home equity gain (from appreciation) covers all transaction frictions plus the extra monthly costs you paid vs renting. After break‑even, owning starts to win financially.

**Nominal** – numbers with inflation included (what you actually see in your bank account).
**Real** – numbers after removing inflation (shows true purchasing power).

---

## What the calculator does step by step

### 1. Monthly unrecoverable cost of owning
We add up:
- Mortgage interest (loan × rate ÷ 12)
- Property tax (home price × tax rate ÷ 12)
- Insurance (annual ÷ 12)
- Maintenance (home price × maintenance % ÷ 12, reduced by your DIY percentage)

Then we subtract your **lifestyle benefit** (if you believe owning gives you emotional value).

### 2. Monthly savings from renting
If the owner’s monthly cost is higher than rent, the renter can save the difference. That monthly amount is shown as **Monthly DCA amount**.

### 3. Break‑even (nominal)
We check month by month how long it takes for the home’s nominal appreciation (price increase) to pay off the transaction frictions plus the extra monthly costs you paid while owning. When appreciation ≥ those extra costs, you break even.

### 4. Investing the renter’s savings in the S&P 500
- **Lump sum** = down payment (because a renter keeps it).
- **Monthly DCA** = the monthly savings from renting (if any).
- We compound both at your chosen annual return (default 9%) monthly.
- **Nominal portfolio** = what you would have in your account.
- **Real portfolio** = nominal value divided by (1 + inflation)^years – shows what it’s worth in today’s dollars.

### 5. Home equity after N years
- We calculate the home’s future value: price × (1 + appreciation)^years.
- We calculate the remaining mortgage balance using standard loan amortisation for a 15‑ or 30‑year term.
- **Net equity** = future home value – remaining mortgage. This is what you would pocket before selling costs.

### 6. Inflation
Inflation reduces the real value of both your investment portfolio and your home. The calculator shows both nominal (actual dollars) and real (inflation‑adjusted) numbers so you compare apples to apples.

## Example with default numbers

- Home price: $430,000, down 20% → loan $344,000
- Monthly owner unrecoverable cost: ~$2,507
- Monthly rent: $2,300
- Owner premium = $207/month → renter can invest $207/month
- Transaction frictions: $37,840
- Home appreciation: 3% per year (nominal)
- Break‑even: about 7 years (after 7 years, appreciation pays off the extra costs)
- After 7 years:
  - Renter’s SPY portfolio (9% return): ~$330,000 nominal, ~$280,000 real
  - Owner’s home value: ~$529,000 nominal, remaining mortgage ~$280,000, net equity ~$249,000

> The renter’s portfolio is liquid (cash). The owner’s net equity is tied to the house. Choose based on your life stage and need for flexibility.

---

## Notes

- All numbers are estimates. Use your own tax and legal advice.
- Selling commission is **not** subtracted from net equity in the display, but it is included in the break‑even calculation. If you sell, commission will reduce your net cash.
- The calculator assumes a 30‑year fixed mortgage by default. You can change to 15 years.
- Lifestyle benefit and DIY handiness are optional – set to zero if not applicable.
